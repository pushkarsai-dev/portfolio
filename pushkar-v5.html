Academic Background

From hog lagoons to mosquito midguts

My research identity was not handed to me — it was discovered through exposure, failure, and redirection. I entered NC State curious about environmental biology and found myself, by junior year, doing mechanistic work on a bacterium with biocontrol potential. The thread connecting these projects is not a subject area but a disposition: find the organism, find the question, follow where it leads.

I began with a project examining cyanobacterial bloom dynamics in hog lagoon effluent — a context that taught me the complexity of environmental microbiology and the importance of questions grounded in real ecological stakes. That work led me to the Goller Lab, where I have spent the core of my undergraduate research career investigating Delftia tsuruhatensis and its harmane-mediated biocontrol activity, culminating in a poster at ASM Microbe 2024.

Most recently my curiosity has extended to malaria transmission biology — specifically the mosquito midgut environment after a bloodmeal and how it might be modulated. DRAFT — expand with your current mosquito project framing

The obligation to teach well

Serving as TA for BCH 451 (Biochemistry) required me to find multiple explanatory pathways for concepts I once took for granted — and forced me to discover that explaining something clearly is a different competency than understanding it yourself.
